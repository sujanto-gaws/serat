01

Scope and reading guide

This document is the requirements baseline for Serat. It restates the product blueprint as verifiable obligations: every requirement carries a priority, a delivery phase, an acceptance criterion and a verification method, and every one of them traces to a user story or is recorded as a deliberate exclusion.

Identifier scheme

FR-AREA-nn for functional requirements, NFR-AREA-nn for non-functional ones, US-nn for stories, A-nn for assumptions and C-nn for constraints. Identifiers are permanent: a dropped requirement is marked dropped, never deleted and never reused.

Priority and verification

CodeMeaning

SShould — v1 is materially weaker without it; may slip one phase
CCould — Included if the phase has room
WWon't — Explicitly out of scope for v1 — recorded so it is not rediscovered

What this baseline does not decide

The performance numbers are targets carried over from the blueprint, not measurements. Phase P0 exists to replace them with real figures; if it does, this document is re-baselined before P1 begins rather than quietly edited.

Assumptions and constraints

An assumption is something believed true that was not verified; a constraint is a decision already made that requirements may not reopen. Each assumption records what breaks if it turns out to be wrong, because that is the part people forget to write down.

Assumptions

IDAssumptionIf it is wrong
A-01Single user, single machineThere is one author per vault. No concurrent multi-user editing is designed for.If collaboration becomes a requirement, the write-back and history design both change materially.
A-02Vault fits on local diskVaults are local folders, not network shares or object stores.Network filesystems may break the watcher and atomic-rename assumptions.
A-03Sync is externalThe user runs git, Syncthing or a cloud folder for multi-device sync.Serat must tolerate files changing underneath it at any moment — hence FR-VLT-04 and FR-VLT-05.
A-04Obsidian remains the reference dialectWikilink, embed and inline-field syntax follow Obsidian and Dataview conventions.A divergence in Obsidian's syntax would require a parser update to preserve NFR-CMP-01.
A-05One developer, part-timeEffort estimates assume solo part-time delivery of roughly 30 weeks to v1.Any phase gate slipping compounds; scope is cut before gates are weakened.
A-06Rust is a learnable costThe Tauri decision commits the core to Rust rather than Go.P0 exists in part to measure this friction before the remaining 28 weeks are committed.
A-07Local embedding quality sufficesA small local embedding model gives adequate semantic recall over personal notes.If not, retrieval leans harder on lexical and graph signals rather than adding a cloud dependency.
A-08Extraction quality is good enoughOpen-source PDF and Office extractors produce text of usable fidelity on the user's real archive.Move 5 in the blueprint exists to test this before the pillar is committed to.
